IIAbout

A T cell biologist who wants science to be true.

My research sits where human physiology meets cellular immunology. I'm a postdoctoral fellow at the Salk Institute for Biological Studies in La Jolla, California, and the question I hope to spend a career answering sounds simple: how does the way we live affect the function of our immune cells? That covers the behaviours we choose (diet, exercise, sleep, social connection and stress) and the rhythms we don't: the daily cycle of night and day, and the slow arc of ageing.

I focus on killer T cells, the immune cells that hunt down infected and cancerous cells. I study how biological rhythms shape what they do, and how to use that to build better adoptive T cell therapies for cancer. Since August 2026 I've been in the lab of Dr Pallav Kosuri, whose group builds nanoscale tools to measure how molecules move and generate force.

I care just as much about whether science can be trusted. Too many influential findings don't replicate, and the incentives of academic research too often reward the wrong things. I write about publishing, peer review, bias and the structure of academic labs on my Substack, Fruit of Eden, and on X. In 2026 I co-authored a published critique of a study on “brain sex” in Theory and Society.

I also want to build. I'm the inventor on a provisional patent application relating to adoptive T cell therapy, and I'm exploring company formation as a solo biotech founder.

Before moving to San Diego in January 2025, I spent a decade at the University of Cambridge, where I took my BA, MSci, MRes and PhD. My PhD was funded by the Wellcome Trust, in the lab of Prof Gillian Griffiths at the Cambridge Institute for Medical Research.
